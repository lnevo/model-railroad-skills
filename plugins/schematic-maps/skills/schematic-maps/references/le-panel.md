# Drawing a Layout Editor panel from the grid

`schematic le OUT.xml --data DIR GRAPH.json:NAME ... [--names NAMES.json] [--panel NAME] [--band TITLE] [--fit WIDTHxHEIGHT]` writes one JMRI Layout Editor panel, a `<LayoutEditor>` element, drawn from the solved grid so it looks like the sheet. It holds track, turnouts, anchors, end bumpers, labels, block contents icons and circle shapes, and the masts the names place. It holds no sensors or block beans. Offer it as a starting panel: it has not been loaded in JMRI for every kind of railroad. Put it into a tables file with JMRI closed, add the turnouts and blocks it names, then run Layout Editor's Check menu (un-connected tracks, un-blocked tracks, non-contiguous blocks) once it loads.

The rules below were checked against the JMRI source (`jmri/jmrit/display/layoutEditor/`, release 5.16): `LayoutTurnoutView`, `TrackSegmentView`, `LayoutShape`, `LayoutBlockManager`, `LayoutEditorTools`, `LayoutTurnout` and their `configurexml` classes.

## What Layout Editor reads

- **Turnouts.** A right or left hand turnout stores its centre, B and C; A is always the centre minus B's offset, so A, the centre and B lie on one straight line. Leg C is free. A wye stores B and C, and A is the centre minus the middle of the two. Crossovers (`ver="2"`) store all four points: A to B is one track, D to C the other. A single crossover's diagonal runs from the middle of A-B to the middle of C-D: down to the right is RH_XOVER, down to the left LH_XOVER.
- **Normal route.** `continuing="2"` means closed sets the route to B; `continuing="4"` means closed sets it to C. So the geometrically straight leg can be B while the normal route is the diverging one.
- **Arcs.** A track segment with `arc="yes" circle="yes"` and `angle` (up to 180 degrees) is a circular arc between its two ends. It is drawn from end 2 counterclockwise on screen through `angle` to end 1, so it bows to the left of the way from end 1 to end 2 as seen on screen. `flip="yes"` swaps the ends, which bows it to the other side. Without `circle="yes"`, the arc is a quarter ellipse and `angle` is not stored.
- **Shapes.** A `layoutShape` of type Closed whose points are all Curve is drawn as quadratic curves through the middles of its sides. Eight points on a regular octagon draw a circle.
- **Widths.** Main and side track take their widths from the panel's options: `mainlinetrackwidth` and `sidetrackwidth`, and `mainBlockLineWidth` and `sideBlockLineWidth` in `layoutTrackDrawingOptions`. A segment is main when `mainline="yes"`.
- **Hidden track.** A segment with `hidden="yes"` keeps the track connected but is not drawn.
- **Mast slots.** Discovery and the Dispatcher read a mast's slot, never its icon. An anchor's `eastboundsignalmast` faces the track on its west side and protects the east side; west and east come from the far ends of its two tracks (more than twice as wide as tall: horizontal, west left; more than twice as tall as wide: vertical, west up; otherwise horizontal). An end bumper's one usable slot is the train's direction into it; it protects nothing. A turnout's `signalAMast` to `signalDMast` child faces the track on that leg and protects the turnout's block, or the blocks beyond when that track is in the turnout's own block; crossovers pair A with C and B with D by type. `schematic/slots.py` holds these rules.
- **Size.** `panelwidth` and `panelheight` are the canvas; `windowwidth` and `windowheight` the frame, which JMRI takes as the panel plus 18 by 60 pixels. Zoom is not in the file: it is a per-window user preference, so a new panel name opens at 100% and a name opened before keeps its last zoom and window size.

## How the command builds it

- **Scale.** A grid point (column c, row r) is the pixel point (36 c, 36 r) in its band, moved below the band before it and centred on the widest band. With `equal_width` a narrower band is stretched along its length, at most 2.5 times, with the `band_width` shares as on the sheet. A band in `rotate_bands` is turned end for end.
- **Fit.** `--fit WIDTHxHEIGHT` is the window the panel should open in at 100% zoom. The panel must fit inside it less JMRI's frame allowance of 18 by 60 pixels, so the grid shrinks until the whole panel, margins, labels and mast icons included, fits that: first the row height as far as the height needs, then the column width as far as the width needs, each the largest that fits, so rows keep their room for the labels between tracks. Fonts keep their size. The labels are placed once the grid is settled and never change it (below); when the grid that fits leaves some label or block contents icon no clean spot, the run stops and names it: give a larger window, or turn `block_contents` off. The panel's window is then the panel plus 18 by 60, within the size given. For a display, give its width and its height less the menu bar and a little room for the Dock: 1900x1140 on a 1900 x 1200 screen.
- **Switches.** A switch is a turnout centred on its points, with legs of up to 12 pixels along its tracks. The leg the drawing runs straight on from A is Layout Editor's leg B. When that is the band's leg C, the legs swap and `continuing="4"` keeps the normal route on the band's leg B, or on the leg the names give as normal. Hand is as drawn: C to the right of the way from A to B is right hand. A switch with neither leg straight is a wye.
- **Crossovers.** A double crossover is a DOUBLE_XOVER across its two through tracks, its legs in its four corners. Two switches of the same number joined C to C by one track, both drawn on level tracks, are one single crossover. The two switches' OS blocks go on A-B and on C-D, and the track between them is inside it.
- **Track.** Each track is a chain of segments from leg to leg along the drawn line. Every corner is rounded by an arc of up to 18 pixels radius, tangent to both sides. A turnback is two 90 degree arcs that meet at an anchor on its far point, a half circle as tall as the rows it joins. Hidden track (dashed on the sheet) is `dashed="yes"`.
- **Blocks.** One block per track; with names, a track may carry several, cut in order from its first end to its second. With `lengths` giving the real length of every block of a track (any one unit), the cuts fall at their real shares, so a short throat block (a yard track's West and East ends, sharing the body's sensor) sits at its own end of the track, beside its ladder; without them the blocks are equal, and a track given lengths for only some of its blocks is drawn equal and logged. No block is drawn shorter than 12 pixels, or than the room for the icon of a mast that faces it (8 pixels plus the icon's length). A cut never falls inside an arc: it moves to the arc's nearer end. A cut where a mast stands moves to the nearest straight piece with room for that mast's icon on the side its train comes from (logged when there is none). A turnout carries its OS block.
- **Ends.** A track end is an end bumper. A connector letter or helix track that appears on two drawn bands ends at an anchor, and a hidden segment joins the pair. A helix end gets a circle and `HELIX TO <level>`. A connector end gets its letter in a circle and `TO <level>` above it.
- **Labels.** Band titles and the helix and connector labels are drawn with the track. The drawing also keeps room for switch numbers on the side away from the diverging leg, track names over the longest level run and station names below the band (above it for a station on the top side), at 0.6 of the font size per character: that room is part of the approved geometry (where the bands stand, the panel's size, where the mast icons go), so it stays. The labels themselves are then placed by the label layer below, which changes nothing else.
- **Names.** `--names` is JSON by band key (the first word of the band title):
  `{"Main": {"turnouts": {node: turnout name}, "os": {node: OS block}, "normal": {node: "B" or "C"}, "blocks": {strand id: [block, ...]}, "lengths": {block: real length}, "leg_b": {node: "B" or "C"}, "idents": {...}}}`.
  Node and strand ids are the band's own. Without names, a track's block is its name, or the two switch numbers it joins, made unique. A turnback, and a connector or helix track drawn on two bands, keep one block. An OS block is `OS <number>`, and turnouts are not named.
- **Masts.** A band's names may hold `"masts": [{"mast": name, "facing": block, "protects": [block, ...], "kind": "anchor" | "end bumper" | "turnout", "hidden": true | false, "icon": [w, h], "or_join": true | false}]`. Each goes in the slot of that band that resolves, by the rules above, to the facing and protected blocks given (`protects` empty: an end bumper), and of the kind given if any. Give the blocks as the old panel resolves them, and the masts keep their Signal Mast Logic and Dispatcher meaning. A buffer-stop mast (`protects` empty) with `"or_join": true`, whose track has no bumper because the panel joins it to another band, stands on the join's anchor instead (an anchor, not an end bumper), facing the same block and protecting the join's; the log says so. For that anchor to be a block boundary, give one band of the pair `"joins": "other"`: its hidden joins then carry the other band's block, not its own (both bands asking stops). A mast with no such slot is not placed; the summary counts it and the log says why. Its icon (`signalmasticon`, level 9) sits on the right-hand side of the train it governs, before the boundary, turned to the way of travel (90 east bound, 270 west bound, along a slope on a slope); `icon` is its upright size, 12 by 17 for a dwarf by default. Where the boundary and the icon go is the next section.
- **Idents.** `TOG<n>` turnouts, `TG<n>` segments, `AG<n>` anchors, `EBG<n>` end bumpers and `SHG<n>` shapes. They are unique in the panel and never start a segment with `TO`.
- **Keeping an older panel's idents.** To make the panel a drop-in for an existing one (scripts, audits and contracts that name its objects), a band's names may hold `"idents": {"turnouts": {switch node: ident}, "boundaries": [{"blocks": [block, block], "ident": ident}], "ends": {block: ident}, "segments": {block: ident}}`. Once the track is drawn, the switch's turnout (both switches of one crossover may give its one ident), the anchor between the two blocks, the end bumper that ends the block and the one segment of the block take those idents: the panel XML and every log line use them, while the Panel object's own lookups keep the generated idents: the idents of its turnouts, segments and points (the `points` keys), `masts[*].ident`, `turnout_of`, `strand_segments`, `turnbacks`, `joins`, `join_ends`, `gantries`, `anchors_moved` and `anchors_from` (a script reading them maps one through `P.name(ident)`). `P.log`, the reasons in `P.unplaced` and `P.moved`, and the errors the panel stops with name objects by their written idents. Writing the XML checks that every object goes out under one ident only it has. A boundary must be drawn as exactly one anchor and an end as exactly one bumper, or the run stops; a block drawn as several segments keeps generated idents for them, and the log says so. Each ident must be one JMRI looks up as its kind of object (LayoutEditorFindItems.findObjectByName): a turnout `TO...`; an anchor or end bumper `A...`, `EB...`, `EC...` or `F<n>-A-<n>`; a segment `T...` (not `TO`, `TUR` or `TRV`) or `F<n>-S-<n>`. Each is used once across all bands (the two switches of one single crossover share theirs), may not have the generated form (`G` after the prefix), so given and generated idents never meet, and every block an ident section names must be one the band's `blocks` or `os` give. All of this is checked before anything is drawn. The panel's own hidden join segments keep generated idents and do not count against a block's one segment. Read them off the old panel: the turnout of each switch, the anchor at each block boundary and the bumper at each block's end.
- **Keeping leg letters.** Layout Editor draws a plain turnout's leg B straight on from leg A, and the panel puts the drawn straight leg on B. An old panel may have its leg B on the leg the new drawing bends; anything keyed on leg letters (mast and sensor slots, block paths) then breaks. `"leg_b": {switch node: "B" or "C"}` names the band leg that becomes Layout Editor leg B. When it is the drawn diverging leg, the turnout's centre stands midway between legs A and B, because Layout Editor computes point A as the centre less leg B's offset; legs A, B and C still end where the drawn track starts, and the type (right or left hand) and `continuing` follow the letters. The log names each such switch; its number label still goes by where the legs meet. A switch that is half of a single crossover takes no `leg_b` (the crossover's legs are A to D); naming one stops.

## Where masts stand: the CATS rules on a Layout Editor panel

The rules that place masts on a compact CATS panel ([cats-signals.md](cats-signals.md)) carry over, adapted to Layout Editor, where the slot fixes what a mast means and its icon is a picture placed anywhere. `schematic/le_masts.py` holds them. No mast changes its slot, its kind of slot or its facing and protected blocks: after moving anything the command reads the panel again as JMRI does and stops if one did, or if a moved anchor left its line, passed the end of its track or left a track under its minimum length.

- **Gantries.** Masts of one control point that govern one direction form a gantry when they stand within 2 columns, first to last (a yard's block cuts, made at equal lengths, stagger by up to about that). The control point is the band's `control_points` entry (layer 1) holding the switch the mast governs: its own turnout for a turnout-leg mast, else the first turnout along the track it protects; a switch in no entry is its own control point. Their boundary anchors slide along their own straight track, level or sloped, so the boundaries stand in one column (one x). An anchor stays between the same two points and so between the same two blocks, moves at most 3 columns, and leaves each of its two tracks at least a column long (or as long as it was, if shorter) and the room for the icon on the side the train comes from. Anchors move one at a time, each against where the others stand by then, so two on one track never meet or pass. A turnout-leg or end-bumper mast cannot move, but counts toward the column. An anchor holding masts of two gantries stays. The column is the one most masts reach, then the one where their icons stand clean in their first spot, then the least movement. The masts that cannot reach it may form a column of their own the same way. The panel keeps its width. Block lengths in the tables are not read from the drawing, so they do not change.
- **A mast alone** (in no gantry) on an anchor slides the least it must only when no spot below gives its icon a clean place where it stands: the last resort, as a lone CATS mast moves out of a switch's cell. A mast in a turnout leg stays on that leg, as JMRI binds it there: only its icon moves.
- **The icon's spot.** On the right-hand side of the train, its near side 4 pixels from the rail (the middle of a dwarf about 10 pixels out), turned to the way of travel (along the slope on a slope; up or down the panel at a turnback, the way the train runs there), 4 pixels before the boundary. Other spots tried: further back along the straight track before the boundary, 2 pixels at a time, up to 40 more; touching the rail (1.5 pixels); and 8 pixels out, which suits a curve. Each spot is scored where JMRI will draw it (the whole-pixel place stored in the file), and the cheapest taken:

| Against a spot | Cost | Why |
|---|---|---|
| over a turnout's legs, or within 8 pixels of its centre | 20 | that is the turnout's control: a click there throws it |
| its middle nearer another track than its own | 15 | it would read as that track's mast |
| a track line through it | 10 each | |
| over another icon, a label or a shape | 10 each | |
| touching a line (under 3 pixels from its middle) | 4 | |
| 8 pixels out instead of 4 | 3 | |
| standing further back | 1 per 8 pixels | the mast stands at its boundary |

So touching the rail costs less than reading as another track's mast. A gantry's icons take one spot (the same distance back and out) when that spot is clean for every one of them, so they stand in a line; otherwise each takes its own. Icons are placed in two passes, the second against all the others.
- **Labels go around the icons.** The icons are placed against the room the drawing keeps for its labels, and a track's name in that room slides along its run out of an icon's way; the label layer then places the real labels around the icons (next section).
- **Not clean.** A diverging leg drawn as one short slope from the points has no straight run long enough for the icon, so it may touch the level track beside it (the LE form of the CATS rule for a gap mid-slope in a switch's cell). The log says "no clean spot" with the reasons for each such mast. Each move is logged too: "its boundary moved N px along its track", "its icon stands N px back".

## Labels and block contents icons

`schematic/le_labels.py` places them last, once the grid is settled and the mast icons stand, and changes nothing else: the run notes every turnout, track segment, anchor, end bumper, shape and mast icon and the panel's size, places the labels, and stops naming anything that differs. The approved geometry is fixed; labels adapt to it. In this order: a switch number for each turnout, a name for each named track, a block contents icon for each block, and the station names (the `stations` preference). Placement is free: each item takes the first clean spot of a ranked list near what it names, since tracks may be short or curved and no one side always works.

- **Sizes.** Every box is the one JMRI draws (`PositionableLabel`: the font's `stringWidth` by `getHeight`, its stored whole-pixel x and y the top left). `schematic/jmri_text.py` holds the Dialog font's character widths and line heights as Java 17 draws them, the larger of macOS and Raspberry Pi OS, sizes 8 to 28; every label is written with `fontFamily="Dialog" fontname="Dialog"` so the table is the font drawn. A size outside the table stops.
- **Block contents icons.** A `BlockContentsIcon` shows the train in its block and is empty until one is there, so its box is reserved for `block_contents_text` ("OCS-1234" by default: eight characters, 68 pixels at 13 plain). There is one per block on the panel: a track block's beside its own track, first on the side the track's name did not take, or, only when no spot beside the track is clean, on its own block's track (its middle on the rail, from the middle of the block outward; there its own block's line is no obstacle, everything else still is; each one logged); an icon with no clean spot at the full reserve in any order of placing tries again, beside then on its track, with the `block_contents_short` reserve ("OCS-1" by default, 44 pixels), each one logged with its block and reserve; an OS block's around its turnout, clear of the control circle (each half of a single crossover has its own OS block, around its own half). Written as `<BlockContentsIcon blockcontents="<block>" x y level="4" ... size="13" style="0" justification="left" selectable="no" fontFamily="Dialog" fontname="Dialog" class="jmri.jmrit.display.configurexml.BlockContentsIconXml"/>`, with no background. JMRI 5.16 (`MemoryIcon.displayState`) shows a block's value as text and, while the block has no value, its default icon (a small red X); a background would paint the icon's whole box even with no train in the block, over the rail for one on its track, so none is set.
- **Spots.** A switch number tries above and below its turnout (first on the side away from the diverging leg), centred and shifted a quarter and half its width, then left and right, from 3 to 72 pixels out. A track item tries above and below its track, then left and right, 4 to 43 pixels from the line, from the middle of the track outward 8 pixels at a time, centred on the spot or reaching on from it; a block's icon may at last stand up to 40 pixels past its own block, beside the rest of its track (a short throat block at a crowded ladder). A station name stands 16 to 72 pixels under the band (over it for side `top`), sliding up to 240 pixels along it.
- **Clean.** A spot is clean when its box lies inside the panel (right of and below the margin, within its width and height); is 3 pixels from every drawn track line and turnout leg and 8 pixels from every turnout's centre (the control circle, where a click throws it); is 2 pixels from every other label, block contents icon, mast icon and circle; and reads as its own: a track name or track block icon is no nearer any other line than its own track, and a switch number or OS icon no nearer another turnout than its own.
- **Crowding.** Items that found no clean spot are placed first in another round, up to eight rounds (stopping when an order repeats), so a crowded spot goes to the item with the fewest choices. An item still without one stops the run with an error naming it and counting why its spots were not clean. A spot more than 12 pixels from the item's first is logged.
- **Check.** After placing, every label, block contents icon and mast icon is checked again from scratch: no two overlap, and no label or icon lies on a track line, a turnout leg or a control circle. Mast icons among themselves and against band titles and end labels are judged by the mast rules, which log an icon with no clean spot.
- **Options.** A band's or the page's `le_labels` (the band's keys over the page's; unknown keys and wrong types stop before anything is drawn): `switch_prefix` (default none; "SW-" writes SW-13), `switch_size` (9), `switch_bold` (true), `block_contents` (true; false writes none), `block_contents_size` (13), `block_contents_text` ("OCS-1234"), `block_contents_short` ("OCS-1", narrower than the full reserve), `names_last` (a band's own le_labels only, false; true places the band's track names after every icon and leaves out, with a log line, a name with no clean spot: for a crowded band where each block's icon matters more than its name).
- **Bands and turnouts drawn twice.** `le.panel(..., labels_by_band=True)` places each band's labels in rounds of their own, top band first, so a band's labels are the same however the bands below it are drawn or crowded. `le.panel(..., gap=px)` (default 96 px; or a list, one per pair of bands, top first) sets the space between bands, and `P.band_boxes` holds the box (x0, y0, x1, y1) each band was placed by: its drawing with the room kept for its labels, so a caller can add its own items under a band. The page's `band_align` (`{band key: "left", "centre" or "right"}`, as on the sheet; centred by default) puts a band against an edge of the widest, so a band drawn with others can keep the place it has alone. `band_align` may name any band the page's `band_order` lists, drawn or not (so `--band` can draw part of a full page); a name that is no band stops. A turnout name in a band's `shared_turnouts` may be given to two turnouts (each band that draws one lists it): one turnout drawn twice, such as a crossover whose halves stand on two bands, which JMRI throws together. This is checked once, from the names, before anything is drawn: a name on more than one turnout must be listed by every band that gives it, a shared name is on two turnouts (never more), or on one when a band the page knows is not drawn (the log says so), and the drawn panel must carry each name as often as the names give it. A track end joined to another band is an anchor numbered among the end bumpers (`AGE<n>`), so joining two bands renumbers nothing else of either.
- **Crossovers drawn at one angle.** `le.panel(..., crossover_run=px)` (a number above zero for every band, or `{band key: px}` for some; a panel at a given grid only, not with `fit`) draws every single crossover's diagonal that many px across, its two halves' points that far apart along their rows, so all cross at one angle however each band is stretched. A crossover with no room for it (each half's track, past the far switch's leg, keeps its least block) takes the widest that fits and the log gives its angle; with no room for any crossover JMRI can draw it is drawn as two plain turnouts on its one turnout name (logged). A double crossover's diagonals run the same run across, shorter or longer than as drawn (its corners at 4/3 of the run from its centre, as JMRI draws it), within each leg's first piece less 12 px; where there is less room it takes what there is and the log says so (its far switch's leg is not reserved: a track left shorter than its switches' legs stops the run). Each crossover's points are whole pixels; a piece that rounding takes past its room by under 1.5 px is logged, by more (or past its track's far end) the run stops.
- **Tight and split crossovers.** `tight_crossovers` (`{band key: [[switch, switch], ...]}`, needs `crossover_run`): two single crossovers sharing a track (a three-way switch's two crossovers) drawn as close on it as their shapes allow, the track between keeping only its block's least length; each slides toward the other. When one of the two has no room and is drawn as turnouts, the other is bounded by those turnouts' legs. `split_crossovers` (`{band key: [switch, ...]}`; with or without `fit`): single crossovers drawn as two plain turnouts on their one turnout name, joined by their diagonal, so each track's throat is one point; the half on a track shared with a tight partner (which must be split too), else the top half, stays and the other slides along its row so the diagonal runs `crossover_run` across. A moved half that would leave its track that way under a leg and the least block, or slide past its far end, stops the run naming the switch.
- **Columns, lamps and numbers.** A band's `le_labels` may hold `name_columns` (groups of track names whose labels stand on one centre x, the middle of where the group's tracks all run level side by side; each such name tries only that x, above then below its track, and stops if it has none), `lamp_columns` (groups of track names whose caller's lamps, `lamp_size` [w, h] px, stand on one centre x on each track, on its level piece there, which must exist or the run stops naming the track; kept clear before anything is placed and given back in `P.lamps`), and `numbers_below` (switch numbers, no prefix, whose number tries below its turnout first).
- **Labels band by band.** `le.panel(..., labels_by_band=True)` places each band's labels in rounds of their own, top band first, each band's standing for the next, so a band's labels are the same whatever bands are drawn below it.
- **A caller's items.** `le.panel(..., extras=[{"id": text, "block": block, "size": [w, h], "x": px}])` (`x` optional: the box's left edge, a number of px; the item then tries only spots with its left edge there) asks the label layer to place items the caller draws itself (a Dispatcher station cluster, say), each by its block as a block contents icon is: beside its track (around its turnout for an OS block), on its own block's track at last, never shorter. They are placed before the block contents icons, kept clear of everything as the icons are, checked with them, and kept in `P.extras` (id, block, xy, box, on_track); the plugin writes no XML for them. Ids must be unique and blocks drawn on the panel, or the run stops before placing; no clean spot stops naming the id.
- **What the panel keeps.** `P.labels` (each with `kind`: title, end, switch, name or station, and `box`) and `P.bcis` (block, kind track or os, xy, size, box, on_track), every box `(x, y, width, height)` as the mast icons' `box`, so a script adding more to the panel can keep clear of them.

Keep the schematic as the master. Regenerate the panel from it rather than editing the two by hand.
